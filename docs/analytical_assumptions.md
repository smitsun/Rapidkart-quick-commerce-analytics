# Analytical Assumptions and Limitations

## Data provenance

Every record is generated by `src/generate_data.py`. No customer or company record represents a real person or organisation. The random seed makes the dataset reproducible.

## Experiment

Control and treatment assignments are shuffled independently of order characteristics, with final assignment counts constrained so small simulations remain balanced. The treatment is designed to improve conversion from approximately 28% to 32%, while converted treatment orders receive a small additional discount. The business decision must therefore compare incremental contribution, not only conversion lift.

## Retention

The generator increases the time to a customer's next order—and sometimes ends future purchasing—after a late, cancelled, or refunded order. The analysis measures association using a 30-day repeat window. In a real company, causal estimation would require controls for customer propensity, geography, time, inventory availability, and other confounders.

## Contribution margin

Estimated delivery cost is a simulated variable cost based on distance, weather, and rush-hour pressure. Fixed corporate, technology, and warehouse costs are excluded. Contribution margin is therefore not operating profit.

## Forecast

The forecast is an ordinary least-squares baseline with trend, weekday indicators, and weekly seasonality. A production workflow should compare this baseline with time-series and machine-learning candidates using rolling backtests.

## Inventory

Daily inventory balances reconcile exactly. Stockout minutes represent intraday unavailability and can coexist with positive closing inventory after replenishment.
